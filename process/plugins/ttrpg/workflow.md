## The Work Cycle

```
Think ──> Play ──> Capture ──> Evaluate
  ^                                │
  └────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Think.** World design, session prep, encounter planning. Shape what's coming — arcs, NPCs, consequences of previous player decisions. Often spans multiple exchanges before the session happens.

**Play.** The game session itself. Real-time support: rules lookups, NPC dialogue, improvised encounters. The Game Master adapts to player choices as they happen.

**Capture.** After the session: journal what happened — player decisions, world-state changes, narrative threads, GM observations. Update the KT with new world state (faction changes, NPC status, location discoveries).

**Evaluate.** Does the campaign arc still make sense given what the players did? Is the pacing right? Are there threads that need attention? The Human Lead reviews and decides the next direction.

The cycle is fluid. Think and Play often overlap — the GM improvises world design in response to player actions. Capture and Evaluate often merge — writing the recap naturally surfaces what needs attention next.

## Campaign Arcs as Focuses

Campaign arcs map naturally to focuses:

- **Focus:** "The War of the Silver Coast" — a multi-session storyline
- **Gate:** "The war resolves — one faction wins, players' choices determined the outcome"
- **Mode:** Executing (the arc is in play) or Planning (designing the arc before it begins)

Short arcs are single focuses. Long campaigns may stack focuses — a side quest interrupts the main arc, gets pushed above it, resolves, pops off.

Headless is the natural state between arcs — the GM preps, the world evolves, no specific storyline is being tracked.
